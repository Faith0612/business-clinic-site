Before Business Clinic. Before copywriting. Before any of this.

Let’s go back to 2023, where it all began.

I was just a girl with dreams she was almost too scared to dream about, talk less of actually bringing them to life.

I remember working a job where I was supposed to be a secretary.

But somehow I found myself with a mop in one hand, a brush in the other, running up to the third floor of the building to clean the office, sweating before my day had even properly started.

All that secretary steeze? Thrown right out of the building gate.

But there’s one thing I remember vividly.

My boss’s office.

Sorry. My ex-boss’s office.

There was a particular part of his office where his big dreams and goals were written boldly on the wall.

And every single time I went in to clean, I’d find myself just… staring at them.

I’d try not to look. Because honestly, there was a certain heaviness that came with it.

But there I was.
Brush in one hand, mop in the other, sweating, reading the dreams on that wall.

And I’d think to myself “Even with everything this man has already achieved, he still has dreams. He still wants more”.

Then I’d think about my own.

The things I wanted for myself. The kind of life I wanted to live. The things I wanted to achieve.

Because at that time, my dreams felt too big for me. So far away that I could barely imagine them actually becoming my reality.

I’d stand there reading his goals, and suddenly my own dreams would come rushing back to me.

Then I’d step out of that office, get back to work, attend to whatever needed doing, and just like that… life would swallow those thoughts again.

Until the next morning. When I’d be back in that office with my mop, staring at those same words.

It was really a strange feeling.

Spending my days helping him build his dreams while quietly wondering what it would actually take to build something of my own.

Eventually, something happened that went way beyond what I was willing to tolerate. We’re not going into that story here.

I was given two choices …

DO IT or LEAVE.

And I left.

I remember going home that day literally shaking.

I took a bath, put some music on, danced my heart out, and eventually fell asleep.

Gosh. I had never been that happy.

It felt so refreshing. Like I could finally breathe again.

But when that initial happiness settled, one question was waiting for me.

Okay Faith. What next?

I’d spent so much time dreaming about having my life back. Now I had my time, and I knew I needed to do something with it.

I’d always heard about people making money online but I’d never really committed to figuring out how.

So I started looking.

After weeks of scrolling through the internet, I came across copywriting.

And I went completely down the rabbit hole.

I started learning, watching free resources, trying to understand how the whole thing worked.

And eventually I decided I needed a proper copywriting course.

That’s when reality slapped me.

I was BROKE.

Like, I’d worked at that place for two whole years. Two years.

And I looked at my finances and realised I’d been living from bill to bill with barely anything saved.

What exactly had I been doing with my money?

I’d already promised myself I wasn’t going back to a 9-5.

But I needed that course. And wanting something badly wasn’t going to make the money magically appear in my account.

So with a very heavy heart… I went back to work.

Three months.

I worked for three months just to afford one copywriting course.

When I was done, I went back to learning. Using the resources I had, trying to find my feet, trying to figure out this whole copywriting thing.

It was one of the hardest periods of my life. But it was also shaping me in ways I couldn’t see at the time.

Eventually I started getting clients.

And a few years later, I came across one particular client who completely changed the trajectory of my business life.

She saw something in me that even I hadn’t fully recognised in myself yet.

Yes, I was her copywriter.

But I’d often bring ideas beyond the copy.

I’d look at something and think about what else we could do, or how we could approach things differently.

Funny enough, I didn’t think much of it.

I was just doing the work.

But she saw something in that. And she gave me room to do more.

And then one day I found myself being brought behind the scenes of her business.

Now imagine that same girl who used to stand in her ex-boss’s office, mop in hand, reading the big dreams written on his wall.

That same girl was now being invited into someone else’s business. Not to clean the office. But to help work through the things happening inside it.

If this were a movie, this would be the part where the girl walks into a huge mansion for the very first time, completely mesmerised, hoping she doesn’t do anything to stain the beautiful tiles.

Because that was me.

I was seeing a side of business I hadn’t really experienced before. And I was fascinated.

Because from the outside, a business can look like that beautiful mansion.

You see the 20k+ followers, the testimonials, the revenue screenshots, the four-figure offers.

And naturally you assume the sales must be consistent, the business must be running smoothly, and the person behind it must have everything figured out.

I’d heard people say a business could look successful on the outside and still struggle behind the scenes.

But working with her made it real.

She let me see beyond the numbers people saw online.

I got to see the decisions, the ideas, all the little pieces that went into actually running the thing.

And I wasn’t just watching from the sidelines. I was involved in working through those things with her.

We worked through her four-figure offer, explored ideas, changed her messaging, and worked on buyer automation and processes that needed attention.

We didn’t just sit around hoping for the best.

We did the work.

And eventually, things started moving.

Enquiries came in. Sales followed. The messaging changes were having an effect, and the systems and automations we worked on meant she no longer had to handle certain things manually.

She had more time for herself and her clients.

It was one thing to have ideas about what could be improved in a business.

It was another to work through those ideas, put changes in place, and see things begin to move.

And I got to experience that firsthand.

I saw how looking beyond the obvious thing you thought you needed, and paying attention to what was actually happening in the business, could open up different ways forward.

That experience changed the way I thought about everything.

Because let’s be honest.

Sometimes you have an idea you’re completely convinced will work. You put it out there. And it flops.

You try again. It flops again.

Now you’re staring at your dashboard like it personally wronged you.

You’re wondering what happened.
Was it the offer?
The messaging?
The price?

Did you miss something?

Meanwhile you still have clients to serve, bills to pay, decisions to make, and a whole life outside the business that doesn’t pause just because your sales have decided to misbehave.

We talk about the big wins. The launches that sell out. The months when the revenue is looking beautiful.

But what about everything in between?

What happens when something stops working and you can’t figure out why?

When you’re not sure whether to change the offer, change your content messaging, lower the price, or just keep going?

What if the thing you think you need to change isn’t actually the problem?

That’s one of the biggest things my experience with this client helped me see.

Sometimes, before you decide what to change, you need to understand what’s actually happening first.

And sometimes, you need someone to help you work through it.

Not someone who promises every idea will work on the first try.

But because sometimes you try something and it flops.

Sometimes you have to go back, learn from what happened, adjust, and try again.

Someone who can look at the situation from a different angle, ask questions you might not have thought to ask, and help you figure out what actually deserves your attention next.

You don’t have to carry every part of that process alone.

Three months later, we stopped working together.

And honestly, I was both sad and happy.

Sad because I’d genuinely enjoyed working with her and would have loved to continue.

She was my client, and naturally I wanted that income to keep coming in.

But happy too. Because we’d done the work. Enquiries were coming in. Sales were moving. Things were going forward.

And she no longer needed me for the same copywriting work.

We’d seen the results of what we’d built together. And that meant something to me.

But as I looked back at everything we’d worked through, I started to realise that what she might need next wasn’t necessarily another copywriter.

It was something broader. Someone who could look at the business with her, help her make sense of what was happening, and work through what deserved attention next.

And back then, I wasn’t that person yet.

I was still a copywriter, just beginning to discover that my interest in business was growing beyond the work I’d originally been hired to do.

I didn’t have Business Clinic. I didn’t have a name for what I was starting to recognise.

But I couldn’t unsee what I’d seen.

I’d watched a business owner work through challenges that went beyond copywriting.

I’d seen how different parts of a business connected, how one change could affect another, and how much there was to consider before deciding what to do next.

And I wanted to understand more.

So I started researching. Studying business. Asking questions.

Why would someone with 10k+ followers struggle to turn interested people into paying clients?

Why would someone enquire about an offer, book a call, seem genuinely interested, and still not buy?

Why would an offer that used to sell suddenly start struggling, even when nothing obvious had changed?

And why do we so often assume we need to change the content, lower the price, or create something new, before we’ve even investigated what’s actually causing the problem?

I wanted to understand what was happening beneath the surface.

Not just what a business owner could see in their numbers, but what those numbers might actually be telling them, what they weren’t telling them, and what questions needed to be asked before anything was changed.

And that curiosity became part of the journey that led me to build Business Clinic.

A space for business owners to look at what’s happening in their businesses, make sense of what they’re seeing, and work through what to do next.

Because business isn’t some glossy corner of the internet where everyone’s fully booked with their dream clients, scaling to seven figures, and waking up to beautiful revenue screenshots.

Even the people posting those screenshots can have days when they’re staring at their dashboards like they’ve personally been betrayed. 😂

In business, we’re allowed to suck sometimes, too.

You can be good at what you do and still struggle.

You can have paying clients and still have things you don’t understand.

You can help other people with their businesses and still need another pair of eyes on your own.

You don’t have to pretend otherwise here.

I don’t have a perfect story where everything worked out exactly as planned, and I’m not going to pretend I have every answer.

But I believe you shouldn’t have to pretend you’ve got it all figured out either.

Because there’s a real person behind every business.

And that person deserves to be seen, not just measured by the results.

I’m really glad you’re here.
